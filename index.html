Original Music

Sound from Working Land

Every farm keeps time. Drip emitters tick like metronomes, soil answers like a drum, pruning shears snap in counterpoint, and wind turns whole crop rows into a slow, shifting chord. I recorded those sounds in real fields, nurseries, and packing houses, then built them into music with harmonica and digital instrumentation.

IMovement I

Ten Original Pieces

Each of these ten pieces starts with a single agricultural rhythm and follows it wherever it leads. The steady pulse of a drip line becomes counterpoint; soil and seedling trays become a percussion section; compost, irrigation basins, and aquaponic loops become etudes and variations about cycles that never stop turning. Farmers markets and packing-house shifts bring in the human side: the voices, hands, and teamwork that carry food from field to table.

  1. 01

    Dripline Counterpoint

    This piece draws its structure from the steady, metronomic pulses of drip-irrigation emitters. The repeating water rhythms form the backbone of the composition, creating a counterpoint between natural flow and human-engineered precision. As layers build, the music mirrors how irrigation networks distribute life across a field. The piece expresses quiet efficiency and the subtle beauty of controlled water movement.

  2. 02

    Soil Percussion Study

    Inspired by the tactile sounds of planting and soil work, this piece uses ground impacts, granular textures, and tray taps as its percussive foundation. Each rhythm reflects the physicality of cultivation — digging, pressing, and shaping earth. The composition explores how soil carries both weight and resonance. It evokes the grounded, cyclical labor that sustains agricultural ecosystems.

  3. 03

    Nursery Tools Sonata

    This sonata transforms everyday nursery tools into musical voices: pruning-shear clicks, seedling-tray rattles, and soft brush movements. The piece highlights the delicate choreography of tending young plants, where small motions accumulate into meaningful care. Melodic lines weave through these tactile sounds, suggesting growth and quiet attention. It expresses the intimacy of working closely with living things.

    Wind Through Rows

    Built from harmonica lines blended with filtered field recordings, this piece captures the sensation of wind moving through crop rows. The music drifts and bends like air weaving between plants, creating a sense of openness and breath. Subtle shifts in tone mirror changing weather and seasonal transitions. The piece evokes calm, continuity, and the slow passage of time across farmland.

    Aquaponic Currents

    Inspired by the circulating flows of my aquaponics system, this composition uses water tones, pump hums, and gentle melodic arcs to represent nutrient cycling. The piece moves in loops, echoing the closed-loop harmony between fish, plants, and water. Its textures feel fluid and self-sustaining, reflecting the elegance of ecological balance. It expresses renewal, interdependence, and quiet motion.

    Irrigation Basin Variations

    Structured as a set of variations, this piece reflects the dynamic behavior of basin-scale irrigation — pressure shifts, flow changes, and water redistribution. Each variation introduces a new pattern, mirroring how water behaves differently across terrain and system design. The music moves between calm expanses and energetic surges. Tilth Motifs · mini-album

Concept

Tilth Motifs is a five-track collection shaped from real farm audio gathered during volunteer work. Drip emitters, nursery trays, pruning shears, soil textures, and ambient field sounds form the raw material for each composition. Harmonica lines and digital instrumentation guide these textures into musical form, creating a portrait of working landscapes and the quiet rhythms of cultivation. The album reflects how agricultural environments carry their own musical character and how everyday actions in the field can be transformed into expressive sound.

  1. I

    First Light

    This opening track begins with the soft pulse of early irrigation and the faint movement of soil as morning work starts. Harmonica phrases rise gently, echoing the slow brightening of a field at dawn. Nursery tray sounds add a subtle percussive layer that suggests preparation and anticipation. The piece sets the album’s tone with a sense of calm emergence.
